# Material unification: what should the common workshop recognize?

**Assessment, not an implementation checklist.** This is supporting evidence for the [existing TCon proposal](design.md#tinkers-parts-as-widely-usable-manufacturing-components). Basic Create and TCon are the workshop of a witch, hunter or artificer just as much as a machinist. Two traditions can produce the same useful stock without becoming the same tradition. The decision is about a **particular use**: same inventory item, same material when made into a part, acceptable for one job, or genuinely different. These are not interchangeable decisions.

## Same stock: where duplicated outputs really load

| Family | What Build 90 actually does | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **Obsidian dust** | `kubejs:terrain_recovery/crushing/minecraft/obsidian` makes `create:powdered_obsidian`; `occultism:crushing/obsidian_dust` makes `occultism:obsidian_dust`. `forge:dusts/obsidian` serves Create sturdy sheet, **purple Occultism chalk prepared in the Hexerei cauldron**, and TCon melting. | **Best output unification.** One powder from wheels or spirits. Keep both ways of making it, their yields, and the chalk's other requirements. No new TCon dust/part needed. |
| **Zinc sheet** | `powergrid:pressing/zinc_sheet` and `createdeco:pressing/zinc_sheet` both take `forge:ingots/zinc` in a Create press and specify different outputs. TCon plate casts return the Create Deco sheet. PowerGrid’s capacitor and eight other recipe rows accept `forge:plates/zinc`. | **One sheet.** The same machine/input should not select a provider by recipe order. Prefer the already-cast sheet if all native consumers accept it. A zinc *part material* is an independent decision. |
| **Netherite nuggets** | Both providers have producing recipes, but **only** `tconstruct:netherite_nugget` is in `forge:nuggets/netherite`. Create Deco has its own ingot breakdown; TCon has breakdown and casting. | **One nugget after a value check.** Inspect both compression directions, casts and consumers before aligning outputs; do not merely broaden an input tag. |
| **Knightmetal** | Twilight Forest smelts armor-shard clusters into its ingot; TCon casts its own. Both ingots feed `tconstruct:knightmetal`. Shards are already a different shared TCon material, `tconstruct:knightly`. | **Bench already solved; ingot output still split.** Keep shard and ingot distinct. Existing [clash remediation](../kubejs/server_scripts/zz_recipe_clash_remediation.js) deliberately pairs storage blocks and ingots: reconcile all four directions together or leave the blocks alone. |
| **Silicon** | AE2 smelts certus dust to `ae2:silicon`; OC2R processes its silicon blend to `oc2r:silicon`. Both enter `forge:silicon`; both the silicon press and raw wafer use it. | **Shared stock, conditional on progression review.** The real question is whether the two production costs are meant as alternatives. Don’t merge the *printed silicon, wafer or circuit boards*. No demonstrated need for a silicon tool head. |
| **Copper, silver, ordinary steel; common metal dusts** | Six registered copper nuggets, **one** listed output identity (`tconstruct:copper_nugget`). Silver ingots and nuggets each have two IDs but one listed output; the two raw silver blocks still have separate conversions. Steel's listed ingot is TCon's, not the quarantined Fallout item. Copper/gold/iron dusts each have two tag members but one listed output identity. | **Mostly covered.** Check loot/trades before claiming all old stacks disappear. Do not re-enable quarantined ingots or rewrite raw-block conversions for tidiness. |

Almost Unified configuration alone is not evidence of convergence: zinc plates and obsidian dust are listed, yet their two producers survive in the loaded graph. Conversely, six registered IDs need not mean six stacks from normal crafting.

## Same at the bench, not necessarily at the altar

Malum infuses wool, string and hex ash with spirits; Goety works string, lapis and ectoplasm. Both producing recipes load. Neither input maps directly to a TCon material. | Keep both native rolls, production traditions and specialist recipes (e.g. Malum soul-hunter gear and Goety necromantic gear). No automatic ritual substitution or reverse conversion from binding to either fabric. |

Cobweb-derived `tconstruct:silky_cloth` already has `tinkers_things:silky_cloth`. |
| **Fletching and ordinary bone** | Twilight raven feather already works in a quill and other feather-tag uses but TCon's feather recipe takes exact vanilla feather. **Alias raven feather to existing feather** for fletching; assess Koboleton bone for the existing bone role. | Keep raven-specific magic map focus. ## Which existing consumers would read a TCon material?

**Design boundary, not approval to change these systems:** normalize to standard TCon stats **for the relevant part role**. Decide merges and numbers case by case. Two sources merged into one material give the same part/stats; there is no need for a bespoke magical effect per ingredient. In the pinned TCon data, for example, string’s binding and bowstring stat objects are empty. A valid shared binding can be the whole benefit. A mod consuming the material is a separate decision: show the existing slot or recipe and what it already distinguishes before proposing an adapter. A fixed bill can accept an equivalent input without adding stats, slots or persistence to the result.

| Candidate | Existing distinction; smallest scope worth assessing |
|---|---|
| **Hexerei broom body** | Loaded willow, witch-hazel and mahogany cauldron recipes use their respective logs and different gold/diamond/netherite costs. Hexerei describes slow/middle/fast native brooms; the brush enables flight. A generic `wood` handle cannot recover a tree species. Keep the native logs/tier bills if the distinction matters, or accept a specifically identified equivalent for that body. A new saved handle slot, arbitrary metal brooms or a mining-speed-to-flight-speed formula would be new scope, not unification. |
| **Cold Sweat Sewing Table lining** | The [pack config](../config/coldsweat/item.toml) already varies insulation by installed material: leather, wool, goat fur, hoglin hide and adaptive chameleon molt. It even accepts an optional NBT filter for the ingredient. A material-specific TCon part could map to an **existing** lining profile at equivalent cost. Never match the part item regardless of its material. Rabbit hide and ordinary leather both yield TCon leather but have *different* native insulation; don't infer the lost source or grant the better of both. |
| **Starcatcher rod fittings** | The rod already takes hooks/bobbers: gold, heavy iron and crystal hook recipes are distinct, as are steady and clear bobbers. Audit the **material ingredient of the fitting**; preserve the fitting's native identity, bill and effects. A different hook design or bobber function isn't simply a different TCon stat value. No generic rod tier or forced TCon replacement for the rod. |
| **Ice and Fire creature gear** | Forest/frost/mountain troll leather, desert/jungle Myrmex chitin and differently colored sea-serpent scales select corresponding native armor outputs. Good existing **recipe-level** distinctions. Keep colors/provenance where the native result requires them; one generic chitin plate cannot choose a colony on its own. The dragonbone bow already asks separately for dragonbone, string and wither bone — a possible part-input bridge, not evidence that it reads dynamic limb stats. |
| **Ars mage robes / Goety scythe** | The Ars apparatus takes gold, iron or diamond chestplates in the Sorcerer, Arcanist or Battlemage robes recipes respectively; these are distinct native armor profiles, not arbitrary input armor stats. Goety’s forge ritual upgrades a scythe using three dark ingots, lapis and occult fabric to a native dark-tier scythe. Both already select material grades. If bridging the existing inputs, keep the rites, quantities and native outputs; no generic spellcasting or harvesting bonus. |
| **Magic implements and repairs** | The loaded Ars wand accepts `forge:logs/archwood`; color already needn't select a different wand. Malum’s Soul Hunter armor repair explicitly asks for spirit fabric *and spirits*. Either is a real typed ingredient to review; neither needs a material-dependent spell power or defense system. Keep Malum provenance in the repair if the ritual requires it. |
| **Existing equipment grades** | Sophisticated Backpacks already upgrades a backpack through exact native tiers using iron, gold, diamond and netherite. If supporting TCon stock there, preserve the tier, contents and upgrade transfer. Don’t recalculate capacity from a TCon stat or add a frame slot. |

The smaller system can also be the correct one: **leave a specialist consumer unchanged when TCon identity is too coarse to express its existing choice**. A shared bench identity need not be a universal replacement item. Native material/part identity, a numeric performance difference and a functional attachment are three different things.

## Where **not** to unify globally

- **Magical emerald vs emerald:** the loaded `goety:magic_emerald`, `goety:magic_emerald_craft`, and awakened-block recipes output *vanilla* emeralds/blocks, but the magical item is in uncommon formal proof, witch currency and catalyst tags. The loaded dark altar uses demonic slate, so this is **not** evidence that that altar is blocked. Check which other proof sources and native consumers survive, and whether erasing the crafted magical item is intended. This is the reverse of a missing alias.
- **Liquids and fuels:** both Create Diesel and PNCR appear in `forge:crude_oil`, `forge:diesel`, `forge:biodiesel`, `forge:ethanol`, `forge:gasoline` and `forge:plantoil`. Check a real tank-to-consumer transfer, fuel value and byproducts before picking canonical outputs; do not rewrite stored tanks. Create/TCon/Bumblezone honey already share users. `forge:honey` **also contains royal jelly**. A honey bottle is not grounds to make royal jelly obtainable from ordinary honey. Likewise a fluid rendering/physics tag (e.g. quicksilver under lava) isn't a material identity.
- **Building palettes, food and devices:** two crop crates, limestone slabs, willow doors, charged circuits, or altars are not duplicate workshop materials by virtue of a tag or name. Use shared stock in construction, preserve placed blocks and inventories. Flour, dough, rice, salt and edible fats have food/processing-specific [existing ownership](design.md); a common food ingredient doesn't need tool stats. `forge:ingots/brick` includes distinct building bricks, not six interchangeable materials in all applications.
- **Compressed iron discrepancy:** [recipe-policies.yml](../recipe-policies.yml) says Create pressing only. Build 90 loaded PNCR explosion and pressure-chamber producers plus block unpacking, with no listed Create press item output for this ingot. Resolve intended acquisition before a cast or a material bridge; this audit did not change or validate that policy.

## Evidence and coverage

Build 90, existing `overworld-tele-soak` snapshot **`760cd9da504d86f1ad726194`**; [run receipt](../generated/test-evidence/20261124T200608Z-2515063/overworld-tele-soak/run.json), [local dumps](../generated/test-evidence/20261124T200608Z-2515063/overworld-tele-soak/fixture/server/better-content-server/generated/runtime-dumps/). Read loaded item/fluid registries, tags, recipe edges, and packaged material resources, then checked selected sources, costs and exclusions. No new server/test run or friends' playtest. Latest source edits are not automatically reflected in that snapshot.

- **[Material form inventory](assets/tcon-unification/material-forms.tsv):** 296 nonempty Forge item tags: typed forms such as ingots, nuggets, gears, ores, dusts, plates, rods, wire, storage blocks, plus common substrate families such as hide, leather, stone, planks, wax and fibre where present. 72 have multiple provider namespaces; **39** of those have at least two members with listed item-producing recipes. Includes intentionally varied glass/stone/ore hosts: **39 is not a count of fixes**. AU column is tag-template inclusion minus ignored tags, *not* an assertion a particular recipe was rewritten.
- **[Same-name census](assets/tcon-unification/same-path.tsv):** all **422** registry paths shared across namespaces among **20,315 registered items / 151 item namespaces**. Includes controls such as two `altar`s. Doesn’t find differently named equivalents; use tags, actual shared outputs and role comparisons for those.
- **[Item leads](assets/tcon-unification/item-leads.tsv):** 5,914 items selected by the material-form tags, TCon input membership or workshop material words in registry paths. Gives item-output and direct item-input recipe counts and example IDs. **Not an exhaustive semantic classification**: whole machines and decorative pieces can match; names like beeswax are not necessarily tokenized as wax. Other findings above can come from recipes/loot rather than this filter. A tag-based consumer is not counted as a direct item edge; normalized alternatives are not a bill requiring every item listed.
- **[Bench routes](assets/tcon-unification/bench-routes.tsv):** all 168 loaded `tconstruct:material` recipes, resolved through the snapshot's item tags: 1,017 distinct input IDs across 73 base identities (variants retained). These are material-value mappings, not proof every shape can be made or every material is hand-carvable. **[Definition coverage](assets/tcon-unification/definition-coverage.tsv)** compares 124 packaged definitions to item and material-fluid edges, including 50 material-fluid recipe rows. **44 definitions have neither mapped route**; some are conditional, loot-only, retained, decoration/embellishment or otherwise supplied. Do not add duplicate definitions on that basis. Stats/conditions come from packaged JSON, not an assertion conditions evaluated true.
- **[Cross-provider fluid tags](assets/tcon-unification/shared-fluid-tags.tsv):** all 24 such tags in the snapshot, including source/flowing IDs, visuals and behavior tags. Not all are recipes or candidates for a common tank fluid.
- Runtime `recipes.json` covers **27,185** loaded rows (27,177 normalized, eight partial, zero reported errors); `tags.json` covers 2,595 item tags. Producer counts are distinct recipe IDs naming an item output, **not survival reachability**: unpacking, recycling and chance outputs count; mob drops, trades, ambient gathering, material-fluid casting semantics and code hooks can supply items without such an edge. Provenance on a real stack and server reload behavior need targeted checks, not estimates from names.

**Order for discussion:** (1) verify the first Otherworld harvest and the magical-emerald/proof interaction; (2) decide on spirit fabric as the first shared part, and obsidian dust as the first shared output; (3) review missing conventional-tool families before optional new stats; (4) resolve zinc/nugget/storage cycles and fluid behavior before changing producers. For any eventual change, keep prior item/material IDs readable and old stocks usable; check paid acquisition → part/output → specialist consumer, reverse recipes and saved parts. Nothing here authorizes a registry removal, new stat system or release.
